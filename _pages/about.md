I am a postdoctoral researcher in pulsar astronomy at the University of
Southampton, working with [Prof. Gregory Ashton](https://gashton.uk).
My research focuses on **pulsar timing**, **simulation-based inference (SBI)**,
and **gravitational-wave detection** with pulsar timing arrays.

I am currently focussing on applying machine learning methods to pulsar timing workflows. To do this, I employ simulation-based inference techniques to profile domain timing through which we can accurately model the frequency evolution of pulse profiles. We are extending this work to time domain and generalizing it for any pulsar so that one can take a trained model and apply it on their datasets straightway. This has massive implications for high-precision pulsar timing as we can disentangle the bias in time-of-arrival (ToAs) due to any other chromatic effects and the uncertainties in the profile can be accurately propagated to the ToAs. This work is currently in preparation, but a glimpse of this has been presented in the IPTA meeting 2026.

Prior to this, I graduated from my PhD from the University of Galway in Ireland. My Thesis title was "Examining ecliptic pulsars using the low frequency telescopes". It explored how we can use ecliptic pulsars to study the effects of Interstellar medium and the interplanetary medium such as the solar wind. This work had several implications to the pulsar timing array (PTA) community on how to model the noise in the timing residuals arising due to the heliosphere. In addition to this work, I actively participated in the IPTA data combination with the low frequency data.
